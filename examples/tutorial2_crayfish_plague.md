# Tutorial 2: crayfish plague (ecological interactions)

Repeat surveys of native and invasive crayfish across the UK, with many incomplete visits, used to anticipate when plague will reach the remaining native populations. It shows how missing data and correlated repeat visits reduce the effective sample size.

**Data:** [`tutorial2_crayfish_plague.xlsx`](tutorial2_crayfish_plague.xlsx) · **Shortcut:** in the app, open the **Tutorials** tab and click **Load Tutorial 2**.

> The data are **simulated**. Rivers are fictional; counts and test results were generated by a statistical model.

## The question

> *Will plague carried by invading signal crayfish reach the remaining native populations, and how soon?*

**Context:** the white-clawed crayfish is protected under the Habitats Directive (Annexes II and V; in the UK, the Conservation of Habitats and Species Regulations 2017) and the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 (Schedule 5), is a species of principal importance in England (NERC Act 2006, s.41) and is Endangered on the IUCN Red List. Signal crayfish are regulated as an invasive alien species, and crayfish plague is a disease listed by the World Organisation for Animal Health.

## The data

| | |
|---|---|
| Design | **94 river reaches** in 18 fictional rivers across England (54), Wales (17), Scotland (13) and Northern Ireland (10); annual visits planned 2018–2025 |
| Measurements | White-clawed crayfish and signal crayfish (presence and catch per unit effort), plague test results, distance to the signal crayfish front, calcium, summer water temperature, fine sediment, bank habitat, angling access |
| Missing data | About **35% of planned visits** were not completed and about 30% of plague tests were not done: **40%** of all values are blank |
| Built-in patterns | Signal crayfish spread along rivers and are stopped by weirs; most carry plague; native crayfish disappear within one to two years of plague arriving, except above barriers and in Northern Ireland (no signals) |
| Sheets | `data`, `sites`, `variables`, `app_inputs` |

## Step 1 · Tier 1: problem definition

| App input | Enter | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ecological objective | Ecological interactions | Invader, pathogen and native host together drive the outcome |
| Spatial domain | Transfer – new sites in a similar region | Reaches the front has not yet reached |
| Number of sites (*n*_sites) | **94** | Rows in `sites` |
| Temporal mode | Early-warning forecasting | Anticipating plague arrival |
| Time points per site (*m*) | **8** | Planned annual visits |

Required capabilities: **multivariate response, species interactions, spatial transferability, representation of temporal dynamics, uncertainty propagation**.

## Step 2 · Tier 2: data and analytical constraints

| App input | Enter | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Number of features (*p*) | **11** | 3 interacting species + 8 predictors |
| Correlation between samples (ρ) | High (ρ ≈ 0.8) | Crayfish populations change slowly |
| Level of identification | Species | |
| Missing values (%) | **40** | Calculated in `app_inputs` |
| High noise / detection uncertainty | Tick | Detection differs between survey methods |
| Interpretability | Medium | |
| Computing | Moderate (workstation) | |

**What the app calculates:** 752 planned samples; each reach's 8 visits are worth 1.65 independent samples, so *n*_eff = 94 × 1.65 × 0.60 = **93**; *n*:*p* = **8.5** (band 5–10).

## Step 3 · Tier 3: method filtering and prioritisation

| Class | Methods | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Recommended | Dynamic and multi-species occupancy models | Model colonisation and loss of sites over time, account for imperfect detection and missing visits, and can link species' occupancy |
| ✅ Recommended | Process-based models | Can represent invasion spread, plague transmission and barriers explicitly |
| ⚠️ Cautious use | Regularised VAR, data assimilation, PLS, random forest, shallow neural network | Only partly meet the requirements; some are sensitive to missing data or need longer series |
| ❌ Excluded | Joint species distribution models, hybrid models | Need HPC-level computing |
| ❌ Excluded | Single-species methods (GLM, GAM, state-space models) | Cannot model the interacting species jointly |

## Step 4 · Tier 4: forecast evaluation, uncertainty and reporting

- **Validation:** spatio-temporal block cross-validation; hold out whole reaches and later years together.
- **Metrics:** hit rate, false-alarm rate and warning lead time for plague arrival; CRPS; prediction-interval coverage; skill against persistence.
- **Uncertainty:** separate observation error (missed detections, untested samples) from true change.
- **Plausibility:** flagged, because data-driven methods are retained. Check predicted spread against known barriers and river connectivity.

## Try changing one input

| Change | Result |
|---|---|
| Missing values → **0%** | *n*_eff rises from 93 to 155 and *n*:*p* to 14 (band > 10): the cost of the missing visits |
| Computing → **High** | Joint species distribution models become recommended; hybrid models and transfer learning appear as cautious use |
| Temporal mode → **Near-term iterative** | Iterative updating is now also required; occupancy and process-based models drop to cautious use because they only partly support it |
| Interpretability → **High** | The shallow neural network is excluded |
