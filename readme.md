# Worked example 2: bacterial communities and environmental pressures

This example uses a repeated survey with environmental data to show how sampling the same sites over time, missing measurements and a strict interpretability requirement change the app's recommendations.

**Data:** [`environmental_pressures_example.xlsx`](environmental_pressures_example.xlsx)
**App:** [Biodiversity Forecasting Decision Framework](https://your-app-name.streamlit.app) <!-- replace with your app URL -->

> The data are **simulated**. The water bodies are fictional, and the counts and measurements were generated by a statistical model to look realistic. They do not describe any real site.

---

## The question

> *If phosphorus inputs to these catchments were halved, how would the lake bacterial communities respond?*

This is a question about the **response to a management intervention**. It is projected forward under a **future scenario**, at the **same sites** that were sampled.

## The data

Each of the 40 sites (in 35 fictional water bodies) was sampled four times in 2025: winter, spring, summer and autumn. That gives 160 rows.

| Sheet | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `data` | One row per sample. Grey columns are sample information, green columns are 20 environmental variables, and blue columns are read counts for 150 bacterial genera |
| `variables` | Each column's group, units, description and percentage of missing values |
| `app_inputs` | The values to enter in the app, calculated from the data where possible (yellow cells) |

The 20 environmental variables fall into two groups:

- **Measured each sampling round (13):** air and water temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, conductivity, total phosphorus, soluble reactive phosphorus, nitrate, ammonium, chlorophyll a, turbidity, dissolved organic carbon and 7-day rainfall.
- **Site characteristics (7):** catchment soil phosphate (Olsen P), catchment arable, urban and woodland cover, distance to the nearest wastewater treatment works discharge, maximum depth and altitude.

About 10% of the laboratory measurements are missing, shown as blank cells. Across all environmental columns that comes to about 5%.

The simulation builds in two pressure signals you could look for: cyanobacteria (e.g. *Microcystis*) increase with phosphorus and summer temperature, and gut-associated genera (*Bacteroides*, *Faecalibacterium*, *Escherichia*, *Arcobacter*) increase closer to wastewater discharges.

## Step 1 · Tier 1: problem definition

Open the app, go to the **Tier 1 · Problem definition** tab and enter:

| App input | Enter | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Ecological objective | Responses to environmental change or management interventions | The question is about the effect of cutting phosphorus |
| Spatial domain | Interpolation – same sites or conditions as the data | Predictions are for the 40 sampled sites |
| Number of sites | **40** | Number of different codes in the `Site` column (`app_inputs`) |
| Temporal mode | Long-term scenario projection | Responses under a future nutrient scenario |
| Temporal resolution | Seasons | Sampling interval; not used in the calculation |
| Time points per site | **4** | 160 rows ÷ 40 sites |

The app reports the capabilities the problem needs: **extrapolation**, **mechanistic process** and **quantified uncertainty**. These come from the objective and the long-term scenario mode.

## Step 2 · Tier 2: data and constraints

Go to the **Tier 2 · Data and constraints** tab and enter:

| # | App input | Enter | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Number of features (p) | **20** | The environmental predictors (green columns) |
| 3 | Correlation between consecutive samples | Moderate (ρ ≈ 0.5) | Seasonal samples from the same lake are related, but change with the season |
| 4 | Sequences assigned to species level (%) | 0 | The data are at genus level |
| 5 | Missing values (%) | **5** | Blank environmental cells, rounded to the nearest 5% (`app_inputs`) |
| 5 | High measurement noise | Leave unticked | |
| 6 | Interpretability | High (regulatory / policy) | Results would inform catchment management decisions |
| 7 | Computing | Moderate (workstation, 8–32 GB) | |
| – | Independent dataset available | Leave unticked | |

### What the app calculates

| Value | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Samples (n) | 160 | 40 sites × 4 rounds |
| Effective samples per site | 1.94 | Four correlated seasonal samples carry the information of about two independent ones (ρ = 0.5) |
| Effective n (n_eff) | 73.7 | 40 × 1.94 × 0.95 (5% missing) |
| n_eff : p | 3.69 | 73.7 ÷ 20 |
| Table B1 band | 1–5, moderate high-dimensional | Moderate overfitting risk |

**Key point:** the sheet has 160 rows, but the app treats the data as worth about 74 independent samples, because repeat visits to the same lake are not independent.

## Step 3 · Tier 3: method filtering

The **Tier 3 · Method filtering** tab should show:

| Class | Methods | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Recommended | Process-based ecosystem models | Provides extrapolation and mechanistic process; usable at n_eff : p 1–5 |
| ⚠️ Use with caution | Bayesian hierarchical models | Only partly meets extrapolation and mechanistic needs |
| ⚠️ Use with caution | Ensemble / multi-model forecasting | Lacks mechanistic process; below its preferred band |
| ⚠️ Use with caution | Regularised regression | Lacks mechanistic process |
| ❌ Excluded | SVM, neural networks, deep learning, LSTM, transfer learning | Black-box methods are excluded by the High interpretability requirement |
| ❌ Excluded | GLM, GAM | Need n_eff : p of at least 5 |
| ❌ Excluded | Hybrid process + ML, PINNs | Need HPC-level computing |
| ❌ Excluded | Random forest, gradient boosting, PLS, ordination | Cannot extrapolate or represent mechanisms |

This illustrates the framework's point that no single method meets every requirement. A process-based model with Bayesian estimation of its parameters would combine the two strongest candidates.

## Step 4 · Tier 4: evaluation and reporting

The **Tier 4 · Evaluation and reporting** tab gives:

- **Validation:** forward-chaining (rolling-origin) validation, which trains on earlier seasons and tests on later ones. With only four rounds this is a weak test, so plan follow-up sampling.
- **Metrics:** error in predicted change or effect size, RMSE and MAE, CRPS, prediction-interval coverage, and skill against a persistence or climatology baseline.
- **Uncertainty:** parameter and scenario ensembles for process-based models, and posterior intervals for Bayesian models. Keep the future scenario separate as its own source of uncertainty.
- **Ecological plausibility:** flagged as a priority, because the predictions go beyond observed conditions.
- **Taxonomic resolution:** a warning that species-level forecasts are not supported.

Download the **TXT report** and **CSV method table** to keep a record of your choices.

## Try changing one input

| Change | What happens |
|---|---|
| Objective → **Community composition**, p → **170** (150 genera + 20 predictors) | n_eff : p falls to 0.43 (band < 1). Nothing is recommended; process-based models and ordination move to caution |
| Spatial domain → **Transfer – new sites** | Bayesian hierarchical models join process-based models as **recommended**, because they share information across sites |
| Temporal mode → **Near-term iterative forecasting** | Time-series methods (state-space models, data assimilation) appear, all with a caution: four time points per site is fewer than they need |
| Computing → **High** | Hybrid process + ML models and PINNs appear under caution, because n_eff : p is below their preferred band |
| Missing values → **25%** | n_eff falls to 58 and n_eff : p to 2.9; the classes stay the same |

Results shown are from app version 4.0.
